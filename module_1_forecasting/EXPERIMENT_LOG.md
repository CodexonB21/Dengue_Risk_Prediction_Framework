## Experiment ID: M1-001

### Date
2026-07-27

### Research Question
For each of the 25 modeled districts, which SARIMA order and count transform
(raw vs. `log1p`) produces the best genuinely out-of-sample walk-forward
forecast, and how well does a per-district SARIMA baseline actually perform
given this project's well-documented zero-inflation and 52-week seasonal
assumption (Decisions 002, 007-011)?

### Districts
All 25 (post Kalmunai -> Ampara merge, Decision 012).

### Data Period
`data/processed/module1/weekly_modeling_table.csv` (25,350 rows, 2006-2026).
Per district: 14 expanding-window annual walk-forward folds (3-year minimum
initial training window, generated by `validation.py`, unchanged) drawn from
all history except the final 104 weeks (2 years), which are held out
untouched for final reporting (Decision 009) and only forecast/scored once,
at the very end, with the already-selected config.

### Stage 1 Model
`pmdarima.auto_arima` (constrained stepwise search: `max_p=2, max_q=2,
max_P=1, max_Q=1, seasonal=True, m=52`) run once per district per transform
on the full pre-holdout history to propose a candidate `(order,
seasonal_order)`; each candidate then walk-forward validated via
`statsmodels.tsa.statespace.sarimax.SARIMAX` refit per fold
(`enforce_stationarity=False, enforce_invertibility=False`), with the lower
aggregate-MASE transform kept per district. See `module_1_forecasting/
MODULE_CONTEXT.md` "Stage 1 Implementation Status" for the 5 approved design
decisions and full per-district table.

### Stage 2 Model
Not built this session (out of scope - `compensation_model.py` remains a
placeholder).

### Features Used
None (Stage 1 is deliberately univariate per Decision 001 - historical
case counts only).

### Metrics
RMSE, MAE, sMAPE, MASE (seasonal-naive scale, m=52), all masking
`is_imputed == True` rows (Decision 011). Ljung-Box (lags 26, 52) on pooled
out-of-sample validation residuals per district.

### Results
- 17/25 districts: `log1p` won. 8/25: raw counts won (`Vavuniya, Kegalle,
  Mannar, Ratnapura, Hambantota, Gampaha, Badulla, Jaffna`).
- 13/25 districts beat the seasonal-naive benchmark on validation (MASE < 1);
  18/25 do on the final holdout block. Best: `Vavuniya` (validation MASE
  0.38). Worst: `Mullaitivu` (validation MASE 2.92).
- Zero `auto_arima` fallback orders were needed across all 25 districts x 2
  transforms.
- **18/25 selected configs have `seasonal_order=(0,0,0,52)`** - no seasonal
  AR/I/MA component at all, despite `m=52`. Both the OCSB and Canova-Hansen
  seasonal-differencing tests independently selected `D=0` for every
  district tested; forcing `D=1` was confirmed computationally infeasible
  at scale (7+ minutes per fit vs. ~0.01s for the `D=0` fixed-order refits
  used everywhere else).
- Zero-inflation % does **not** simply predict SARIMA performance:
  `Vavuniya` (32.2% zero-weeks) is the best performer; `Mullaitivu` (52.7%
  zero-weeks) is the worst; `Colombo` (0.5% zero-weeks, not sparse at all)
  also underperforms (validation MASE 1.63).
- Full per-district table: `models/module1/sarima_selected_configs.csv`,
  `outputs/metrics/module1/sarima_walk_forward_metrics.csv`.

### Residual Diagnostics
Ljung-Box (lag 52) p < 0.05 in 23/25 districts - significant residual
autocorrelation remains in the vast majority of districts. Exceptions:
`Kilinochchi` (p ≈ 1.0) and `Mullaitivu` (p ≈ 0.10). ACF plots for a
representative subset (`Colombo`, `Kandy`, `Mullaitivu`, `Kilinochchi` -
`outputs/figures/module1/acf_residuals_*.png`) confirm this visually:
Colombo's residual ACF decays slowly over ~30 lags rather than cutting off
near zero immediately, indicating substantial unmodeled structure remains.

### Interpretation
The per-district, per-transform, walk-forward-validated selection process
worked as designed and produced a genuine out-of-sample baseline for every
district with no manual intervention needed. However, the AIC-driven
`auto_arima` search systematically failed to find seasonal structure for
most districts (18/25 non-seasonal), which is the most likely explanation
for both (a) the widespread residual autocorrelation (the annual cycle
isn't being modeled, so it leaks straight into the residual) and (b) the
12/25 districts underperforming a naive seasonal-repeat forecast. This
does not appear to be a zero-inflation problem specifically (see Results) -
it looks more like an AIC-vs-forecast-horizon mismatch inherent to
`auto_arima`'s default order-selection criterion at this seasonal period.

### Decision
**Keep** this Stage 1 baseline as the one Stage 2 (residual compensation)
will build on - Decision 010 explicitly requires genuine out-of-sample
residuals to exist before Stage 2 can be built at all, and re-running a
different order-selection strategy is a bigger undertaking than this
session's scope. **Flag** the AIC/seasonal-structure finding (Open Question
#12, `module_1_forecasting/MODULE_CONTEXT.md`) as the top candidate for
follow-up work (STL+SARIMA ablation, or a forecast-horizon-aware order
criterion) before treating Stage 1's absolute performance numbers as final.

  resolved/updated; new Open Questions #12, #13; new "Stage 1 Implementation

  decisions).

## Experiment ID: M1-002

### Date
2026-07-27

### Research Question
Does an XGBoost residual-compensation Stage 2, trained on Stage 1's genuine
out-of-sample SARIMA residuals, measurably and reliably improve forecast
accuracy over Stage 1 alone - and does that improvement differ systematically
between the 18 districts whose Stage 1 SARIMA has no seasonal component and
the 7 that do (Open Question #12)?

### Districts
All 25 (post Kalmunai -> Ampara merge, Decision 012).

### Data Period
Same as M1-001: `data/processed/module1/weekly_modeling_table.csv`, Stage
2's own feature table `data/features/module1/stage2_feature_table.csv`
(25,350 rows), and Stage 1's `sarima_stage1_predictions.csv` (20,800 rows)
as the residual-target source. Stage 2 reuses Stage 1's exact 14 walk-forward
folds and 104-week holdout - no independent fold generation.

### Stage 1 Model
Unchanged from M1-001 (per-district `auto_arima`-selected SARIMA, see
`models/module1/sarima_selected_configs.csv`).

### Stage 2 Model
Pooled XGBoost regressor (`objective="reg:absoluteerror"`, `max_depth=3,
learning_rate=0.05, subsample=0.8, colsample_bytree=0.8, reg_lambda=1.0,
min_child_weight=5`, fixed across folds), one model per walk-forward fold
plus one for the holdout block plus one final production model trained on
all available out-of-sample residuals. `District` included as a categorical
feature (`enable_categorical=True`). See Decision 014 for why MAE (not the
originally-planned squared error) was needed.

### Features Used
Feature Groups 1-5 + 5b (`FEATURE_ENGINEERING_SPEC.md`): case lags/rolling
stats/rate of change, rainfall/temperature/humidity lags (rainfall now
`precipitation_sum`-based per Decision 008), fold-scoped climate anomalies,
`sin_week`/`cos_week`/monsoon indicators, `sarima_prediction`,
`residual_lag_1/2` (full-calendar-reindexed per Decision 015), and `District`
(categorical, Decision 014).

### Metrics
RMSE, MAE, sMAPE, MASE (per fold + median-aggregate + holdout, masking
`is_imputed`), Diebold-Mariano test (two scopes: validation+holdout pooled,
holdout-only), residual variance reduction, final Ljung-Box (lags 26/52) on
`actual - final_prediction`.

### Results
- **24/25 districts** improve on both validation-aggregate and holdout MASE;
  `Kilinochchi` is the sole exception on both (validation -9.4%, holdout
  -4.5%, neither reaching statistical significance). Median % improvement
  across all 25 districts: 42.8% (validation-aggregate), 28.7% (holdout).
- **Diebold-Mariano**: 12/25 districts significant (`p < 0.05`, Stage 2
  better) at the larger validation+holdout scope; 4/25 at the stricter
  holdout-only scope (n=104/district). No district shows a *significant*
  worsening at either scope.
- **Open Question #12 resolved**: the 18 non-seasonal-SARIMA districts show a
  **larger** median improvement (43.2% validation / 37.2% holdout) than the 7
  seasonal-SARIMA districts (28.5% validation / 24.3% holdout) - evidence
  Stage 2's seasonal/climate features are compensating for Stage 1's missing
  annual cycle in those 18 districts, without needing Stage 1 rework.
- **Residual variance reduction**: 21/25 districts positive (up to 82% for
  Trincomalee); 4 negative (`Mannar, Jaffna, Mullaitivu, Kilinochchi`) -
  notably 3 of those 4 still improved on MASE, showing variance reduction and
  MASE can disagree.
- **Major mid-implementation finding**: the first full run (standard
  `reg:squarederror` objective) produced the *opposite* result - 23/25
  districts got worse. Root cause: Stage 1's SARIMA diverged catastrophically
  for `Vavuniya` in one fold (2010 wk42-51, forecasts to ~30M against an
  actual mean of ~6/week), and Stage 2's pooled-model architecture let that
  single extreme residual dominate training globally, corrupting every other
  district's predicted residuals too. Switching to `reg:absoluteerror`
  (bounded gradient, outlier-immune) fixed this. See Decision 014 and Open
  Question #14.
- Full detail: `outputs/metrics/module1/combined_vs_baseline_metrics.csv`,
  `outputs/metrics/module1/diebold_mariano_results.csv`,
  `outputs/metrics/module1/xgboost_feature_importance.csv`.

### Residual Diagnostics
Final Ljung-Box (lag 26, pooled non-imputed validation residuals) still
shows significant autocorrelation (`p < 0.05`) in **23/25 districts** - only
`Ampara` and `Anuradhapura` pass. Stage 2 reduces error magnitude
substantially but does not fully whiten the residual; real structure likely
remains unexploited. ACF plots for the representative subset
(`outputs/figures/module1/acf_residuals_final_*.png`) confirm this
qualitatively. Top feature importances (`residual_lag_1`, `residual_lag_2`
dominate by a wide margin) directly explain why: the prior 1-2 weeks' own
SARIMA error is the single strongest predictor of the current week's error -
i.e. Stage 1's error is itself autocorrelated, and Stage 2's residual lags
are what's being exploited.

### Interpretation
Residual compensation works as hypothesized for the large majority of
districts, with a directionally consistent and often large improvement, but
statistical significance is only reached for a minority of districts at
current sample sizes - an honest, not overstated, result. The pooled-model
architecture (Decision 002 revisited for Stage 2 as Decision 014) is
powerful (enables adequate training data per fold) but introduces a real
robustness requirement: a single district's data-quality problem (here, a
SARIMA divergence) can silently corrupt every other district's correction
unless the loss function is chosen to be robust to it. This is a genuinely
new methodological finding for the pooled-residual-compensation approach,
not specific to this dataset. The seasonal-vs-non-seasonal diagnostic
(Open Question #12) resolves in favor of the original sequencing bet: no
Stage 1 rework is currently justified.

### Decision
**Keep** the pooled, MAE-objective XGBoost Stage 2 as implemented -
`Kilinochchi` remains the one documented exception, worth a closer
district-specific look in future work but not disqualifying for the overall
approach. **Flag** (not fix this session) Stage 1's `Vavuniya` SARIMA
divergence as a genuine data-quality finding (Open Question #14) - median
fold aggregation hid it from Stage 1's own reporting. **Flag** the residual
autocorrelation that survives Stage 2 (23/25 districts) as the next-highest
priority follow-up (additional residual lags, or a different Stage 2
architecture/feature set).

  resolved; #12 resolved with evidence; new Open Question #14; new "Stage 2
  Implementation Status" section with full results table).
- `module_1_forecasting/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` (this entry).
- `research_context/RESEARCH_DECISIONS.md` (Decision 008 rainfall
  sub-decision finalized; new Decisions 014, 015, 016).
- `research_context/FEATURE_ENGINEERING_SPEC.md` (Group 5's leakage-safe

## Experiment ID: M1-003

### Date
2026-07-27

### Research Question
Two related questions pursued in the same session: (1) Can Stage 1's
`Vavuniya`/`Mannar` SARIMA divergence (Open Question #14, flagged but not
fixed in M1-002) be root-caused and fixed, and does fixing it change Stage
2's headline results? (2) Sanity-check prompted by a real-world event: given
the actual, ongoing 2026 dengue outbreak in Colombo/Gampaha, does the
Module 1 pipeline predict it with any real accuracy, and if not, why not?

### Districts
All 25 for question (1); `Colombo` and `Gampaha` specifically for question
(2) (the two districts named in the real-world outbreak).

### Data Period
Unchanged from M1-001/M1-002, except the dataset was confirmed to extend to
**2026 week 25** for case counts (25,350+ rows) vs. only **2026 week 21**
for climate data - a gap discovered during question (2)'s investigation.

### Stage 1 Model
Same per-district `auto_arima`-selected SARIMA as M1-001/M1-002, with one
fix: `fit_and_forecast()` now rejects any fit whose AR polynomial (regular +
seasonal combined) has a root on or inside the unit circle, treating it as a
failed fit (`NaN`) rather than returning an explosive/non-stationary
forecast. See Decision 017.

### Stage 2 Model
Unchanged from M1-002 (pooled XGBoost, `reg:absoluteerror`), re-run against
the corrected Stage 1 outputs. `compensation_model.py`'s training-row
selection and `combine.py`'s `residual_variance_reduction()` were hardened
to correctly exclude/handle the newly-possible `NaN` residual rows (see
Decision 017's Implication section).

### Features Used
Unchanged from M1-002.

### Metrics
Unchanged from M1-002 (RMSE/MAE/sMAPE/MASE, DM test, residual variance
reduction, final Ljung-Box). For question (2) specifically: sMAPE compared
across periods with vs. without complete climate feature coverage.

### Results

**Question (1) - Stage 1 fix:**
- Root cause confirmed precisely: `Vavuniya` fold 1 (2010) fit an AR(1)
  coefficient of 1.266 (explosive); `Mannar` fold 13 (2022) independently
  fit a seasonal AR coefficient of 1.162 (all 52 seasonal roots on the unit
  circle) - confirming this is a general failure mode of
  `enforce_stationarity=False`, not a `Vavuniya`-only quirk.
- Full pipeline regenerated. **Stage 2 headline improved from 24/25 to a
  clean 25/25 districts** improving on validation-aggregate MASE
  (`Kilinochchi` flipped from -9.4% to +5.3%). Median validation MASE
  improvement: 43.5% (was ~42.8%). Median **holdout** MASE improvement rose
  more substantially: 32.7% (was ~28.7%) - the fix disproportionately helped
  holdout-block consistency.
- Holdout win rate is 23/25, not 25/25: `Kilinochchi` (-11.7%, worse than
  before the fix) and `Mannar` (-3.0%, newly negative) are the two
  exceptions, neither statistically significant (DM test `p > 0.3` both).
- `Vavuniya` itself went from one of the pipeline's most fragile districts
  to one of its best (validation MASE 0.375 → 0.286).
- **Bug found and fixed while re-verifying DM test results**:
  `evaluate.dm_test`'s docstring had the `mean_loss_diff` sign convention
  backwards (said `< 0` meant "Stage 2 helped"; the actual code computation
  means `> 0` does). The numbers reported in M1-002/Decision 016 were
  already computed correctly in code - only the prose explanation was wrong,
  now fixed.

**Question (2) - real-world outbreak check:**
- The dataset already includes the actual outbreak (`Colombo` 1,138 cases,
  `Gampaha` 1,294 cases at 2026 week 25) inside the untouched holdout block.
- Stage 1+2 achieves sMAPE of **14-24%** across every period checked with
  complete climate data (Dec 2025: 13.9%/20.1%; all 2025: 15.9%/16.6%; 2026
  weeks 1-21 excl. a week-14 reporting-dip artifact: 17.9%/23.8%) - a
  genuinely useful accuracy level, including through the early ramp-up of
  the real outbreak.
- **Root cause of the apparent week-25 "failure" (~8-10x underestimate for
  both districts)**: the shared climate data pipeline has not been
  refreshed past 2026 week 21, leaving every climate feature (rainfall,
  temperature, humidity, and all lags) entirely `NaN` for weeks 22-25 -
  exactly the weeks containing the spike. sMAPE for those 4 weeks alone is
  ~97% for both districts, a ~4-5x degradation directly attributable to the
  missing inputs, not a modeling failure.
- Separately, even with climate data present, a 104-week-ahead one-shot
  holdout forecast would not be expected to anticipate a sudden 8-10x
  outbreak spike regardless - that is a fundamentally different (and
  harder) task than the rolling 1-week-ahead forecast a real deployment
  would use. Not yet tested this session (flagged as follow-up, Open
  Question #16).

### Residual Diagnostics
Final Ljung-Box (lag 26) still shows significant autocorrelation in 23/25
districts post-fix - `Ampara` and `Vavuniya` now pass (previously `Ampara`
and `Anuradhapura`); `Vavuniya` newly joins the passing set because its own
explosive-fold contamination is gone.

### Interpretation
The Decision 017 fix confirms that Open Question #14's Vavuniya finding was
a genuine, fixable Stage 1 robustness gap, not a data artifact - and that
`Mannar` had the identical undiagnosed problem, found only by a systematic
scan rather than a targeted look. Fixing a Stage 1 robustness bug measurably
improved Stage 2's headline result, which is reassuring: it suggests Stage
2's architecture is not just "hiding" more Stage 1 problems of this kind
elsewhere (a full 25-district scan found exactly these two cases and no
others). Separately, the real-world outbreak check is a valuable reality
check on what this pipeline can and cannot currently claim to do: it
predicts case counts with real-world-useful accuracy when given complete
inputs, but the moment its climate inputs go stale, accuracy collapses -
this is an operational/data-engineering concern as much as a modeling one,
and should be foregrounded in any discussion of "real-time" outbreak
tracking capability.

### Decision
**Keep** the AR-root stability guard permanently in Stage 1 - it is a
strict improvement (25/25 vs 24/25 validation win rate) with no observed
downside. **Do not** yet build the rolling 1-week-ahead re-evaluation
proposed by Open Question #16 - deferred by explicit user direction this
session in favor of prioritizing the Stage 1 fix first. **Flag** the
climate data pipeline currency gap (2026 weeks 22-25) as an actionable,
separate follow-up - refreshing it is a prerequisite before any further
real-time-accuracy claims.

## Experiment ID: M1-004

### Date
2026-07-27

### Research Question
Distinct from every prior experiment (all of which score against data
already in the dataset, just held out from training): can the trained Stage
1+2 pipeline produce a genuine forward forecast for weeks that don't exist in
the dataset at all, and if so, what does it predict for `Colombo`/`Gampaha`
immediately following the real 2026 week-25 outbreak spike (Open Question
#16)?

### Districts
All 25 (mechanism), with `Colombo`/`Gampaha` as the illustrative focus given
the ongoing real-world outbreak.

### Data Period
Same full history as M1-003 (case data through 2026 week 25, climate data
through 2026 week 21). New: an 8-week forward horizon (2026 weeks 26-33) with
no ground truth in the dataset at all.

### Stage 1 Model
Each district's already-selected `(order, seasonal_order, use_log1p)`
(`sarima_selected_configs.csv`), refit once on the district's ENTIRE
available history (not a pre-holdout window) via the unchanged
`fit_and_forecast()`, then forecast 8 steps ahead in one deterministic
multi-step call.

### Stage 2 Model
The existing final production XGBoost model (`xgboost_final_model.json`,
trained on all 14 folds + holdout - unchanged, no retraining this session).
Applied recursively, one future week at a time (new: `forecast_future.py`):
for the first 1-2 future weeks, `residual_lag_1/2` and case-count lags use
real historical values; for every week after that, the script's own
prior-step `final_prediction`/`predicted_residual` are fed back in as the
lag inputs (standard recursive multi-step forecasting).

### Features Used
Unchanged feature set (`compensation_model.FEATURE_COLUMNS`). Climate-derived
features (raw anomalies, and any lag reaching past week 21) are `NaN` for
every future week, by construction, since climate data doesn't extend past
that point (Open Question #16) - handled natively by XGBoost's missing-value
routing, not fabricated.

### Metrics
No accuracy metric is possible (no ground truth exists yet for future
weeks). Instead, a `feature_completeness_pct` diagnostic (share of non-`District`
`FEATURE_COLUMNS` that are non-`NaN`) is reported per row as an honest
per-week confidence proxy.

### Results
- `feature_completeness_pct` declines steadily with horizon across all
  districts: 56.2% (step 1) -> 53.1% -> 50.0% -> 46.9% -> 43.8% (steps 5-8,
  where it flattens once both `residual_lag_1/2` are fully recursive and no
  further climate lags remain in range).
- `Colombo`: predicted cases rise from the pre-spike ~300-500/week baseline
  to 442.7 (week 26), peak at 524.8 (week 27), then settle to a 460-470/week
  plateau (weeks 28-33) - well above the pre-spike baseline but far below the
  week-25 spike value (1,138) itself.
- `Gampaha`: similarly rises from a ~200-500/week baseline to 1,087.7 (week
  26), peaks at 1,465.9 (week 27), then settles to a ~1,360-1,370/week
  plateau (weeks 28-33) - also well above baseline, closer to (but not
  exactly repeating) the week-25 spike value (1,294).
- Both districts show a suspicious week-24 dip (`Colombo` 20, `Gampaha` 24
  cases) immediately before the week-25 spike - a plausible reporting-lag
  catch-up artifact (Open Question #16's third bullet). The forecast's
  plateau-rather-than-continued-spike shape is consistent with the model
  correctly not over-extrapolating a possibly-artifactual single-week outlier,
  though this has not been confirmed against raw source data.
- Full output: `data/processed/module1/future_forecast.csv` (200 rows = 25
  districts x 8 weeks). Plots:
  `outputs/figures/module1/future_forecast_{Colombo,Gampaha}.png`.

### Residual Diagnostics
Not applicable - no observed residual exists yet for future weeks.

### Interpretation
The pipeline can produce a directionally sensible forward forecast beyond
the dataset's end: both outbreak districts are correctly predicted to remain
substantially elevated relative to their pre-outbreak baseline, without
naively repeating the single week-25 spike value (which may itself be
partly a reporting-catchup artifact). This is qualitatively different
evidence from the holdout MASE numbers (M1-002/M1-003) - it is a
demonstration of the pipeline's forward-looking capability, not a validated
accuracy claim, and should never be cited alongside the holdout results as
if it were equivalently rigorous. The declining `feature_completeness_pct`
is the honest, quantified reason why: by week 3 of any forward forecast, the
two most important Stage 2 features (`residual_lag_1/2`, per M1-002's
feature-importance finding) are already self-fed rather than real, and
climate features are entirely absent throughout.

### Decision
**Add** `forecast_future.py` as a new, clearly-separated deliverable
(production forward forecasting) - not merged into `main.py`'s validated
walk-forward/holdout orchestration, since it answers a fundamentally
different question with a fundamentally different (and lower) evidence
standard. **Do not** treat `future_forecast.csv` as a substitute for closing
Open Question #16's climate-data-currency gap or for the still-not-built
rolling 1-week-ahead re-evaluation - both remain open, higher-rigor
follow-ups.

  Forecast" section).

## Experiment ID: M1-005

### Date
2026-07-29

### Research Question
After closing the climate gap (weather through 2026 Wk25), can we fix the two
main operational failure modes behind poor 2026 outbreak-week performance: (1)
reporting-lag dips poisoning case lags, and (2) flat 104-week SARIMA holdout
misrepresenting weekly refit deployment?

### Districts

Not applicable to this entry (DM test only).

itself trained on out-of-sample residuals spanning almost the entire

DM test too, and more starkly than expected. The **honest, new finding** is
that Stage 2's benefit looks considerably weaker and less consistent under
this deployment-faithful weekly-refit mode than the validated walk-forward/
holdout backtest suggests - worth real further investigation, not something
this entry resolves.

### Decision
**Report, do not act on prematurely.** This is evidence-gathering only - no
model, feature, or threshold is selected from it. Flagged as a new, higher-

holdout-mode) rather than folded into the original "thin DM significance"
framing, which turned out to be the wrong lens for this result.

### Date

### Research Question

### Stage 2 Model
Two scoring modes compared on the IDENTICAL already-computed rolling

used), (2) a new fold-matched resolver
(`scripts/diagnose_rolling_dm_gap.py::build_fold_resolver_factory`) that

Unchanged.

Per-district: mean/median absolute SARIMA drift (weekly-refit minus fold-

### Results
- **Weekly-refit and fold-refit SARIMA predictions are barely correlated**:
  mean correlation across districts = **0.13**, with several districts near
  zero or even negative (`Kegalle` -0.14, `Gampaha` -0.03, `Kurunegala`

  with Stage 2 helping (`mean_loss_diff > 0`) dropped from 10/25 (frozen

  `Kalutara` 0.39) - bigger weekly-vs-fold SARIMA disagreement tracks with
  bigger rolling-mode forecast error.
- Full tables: `outputs/metrics/module1/rolling_dm_gap_diagnosis.csv`,
  `outputs/metrics/module1/rolling_one_step_dm_test_fold_matched.csv`.

### Residual Diagnostics
Not applicable (DM test / correlation diagnostics only).

### Interpretation - hypothesis (b) confirmed, (a)/(c) ruled out
Using the "correct," fold-appropriate Stage 2 model instead of the single
frozen final model did **not** recover the holdout-level improvement - if
anything it made the DM test's significance picture worse. This rules out
(a)/(c): the frozen final model's generalization is not the dominant
problem. Instead, weekly-refit SARIMA predictions are so weakly correlated
with fold-refit SARIMA predictions for the same historical weeks (mean
r=0.13, several districts near zero) that Stage 2 - whichever checkpoint
scores it - is effectively being asked to correct a materially different
Stage 1 signal than the one it was trained against. This is a real,
substantial instability in the weekly SARIMA refit itself, not a Stage 2
weakness. A plausible mechanism (not yet tested): the fixed
`(order, seasonal_order)` per district was selected once on the full
history and is refit with `enforce_stationarity=False`/
`enforce_invertibility=False` (already documented, Decision 017, as prone
to occasional explosive/unstable fits) - refitting the same fixed order
on a training window that grows by only 1 week each time may let the MLE
optimizer land on meaningfully different local optima week to week, in a
way that annual-fold refits (much larger relative window changes between
refits) do not exhibit to the same degree.

### Decision
**Report as the root cause, do not fix this session.** This is diagnostic
evidence only - no model, feature, or refit cadence is changed here. The
actionable implication - weekly SARIMA refitting may need a more stable
cadence (e.g. monthly/quarterly) or parameter smoothing across refits
before rolling-mode/production nowcast numbers can be trusted at face value
- is flagged as a new, separately holdout-gated follow-up decision, not
adopted automatically. This also means the rolling evaluator's own
deployment-fidelity numbers (M1-005, M1-008) should be read with this
instability in mind, not just as "Stage 2 doesn't transfer well."

## Experiment ID: M1-013

### Date
2026-08-04

### Research Question
M1-011 (Decision 035) found weekly-refit SARIMA predictions are barely
correlated with fold-refit SARIMA predictions for the same historical
weeks (mean r=0.13), and hypothesized this could be the SARIMAX optimizer
landing on different local optima week to week, given how little the
training window changes between consecutive weekly refits. Does seeding
each week's fit with the PREVIOUS week's converged parameters
(`start_params`) reduce this instability?

### Districts
`Kilinochchi` (fast/sparse) and `Colombo` (thesis-critical, highest
measured drift in M1-011) for an initial cheap check, before committing to
a full 25-district run.

### Stage 1 Model
`baseline_sarima.fit_and_forecast()` extended with optional `start_params`/
`return_params` keyword arguments (default behavior byte-identical when
unset); `rolling_one_step.rolling_one_step_district()` extended with a
`warm_start` parameter that chains each week's fitted params into the
next week's `start_params`. Both changes are additive and backward-
compatible - no existing caller's behavior changes.

### Stage 2 Model
Not touched.

### Features Used
Unchanged.

### Metrics
Per-fit wall-clock time; correlation and mean absolute difference between
{cold-start, warm-start} predictions and the fold-refit SARIMA prediction
for the identical `(District, Year, Week)`, on a 60-week sample per
district (cheap decisive check, run before any full-scale evaluation).

### Results
- **Per-fit cost**: warm-started fits took **2.4-10s each** vs. the
  cold-started baseline's **~0.6s** (a 5-10x slowdown) - a real,
  non-trivial cost discovered during smoke testing, not anticipated when
  the approach was proposed.
- **Warm-started and cold-started predictions are virtually identical**:
  e.g. Colombo week 2 of the sample: cold=80.250588, warm=80.251638 (differ
  in the 3rd decimal); correlation with fold-refit SARIMA is unchanged to
  4 decimal places for both districts (`Kilinochchi`: cold r=-0.0518, warm
  r=-0.0517; `Colombo`: cold r=0.1809, warm r=0.1809). Mean absolute
  difference from fold-refit is likewise unchanged (`Colombo`: 61.246 cold
  vs 61.246 warm).
- Given this cheap 60-week check fully falsified the hypothesis with no
  ambiguity, **the planned full 25-district rolling re-run was not run** -
  it would have cost ~2+ hours (5-10x the ~40 min budgeted, since warm-
  started fits are that much slower) for a result already clear from a
  much smaller, faster sample. Not running it is a deliberate, disclosed
  scope decision, not a silent gap.

### Residual Diagnostics
Not applicable.

### Interpretation
The `SARIMAX` optimizer converges to essentially the SAME solution
regardless of starting point for this model/data combination - it is
already robust to its starting point, just less computationally efficient
when given an explicit (as opposed to its own internally-computed) one.
This means the M1-011 finding (weekly-refit SARIMA is only weakly
correlated with fold-refit SARIMA) is **not** an optimizer-local-optima
artifact fixable by warm-starting - the instability is a more fundamental
property of how the true MLE optimum itself shifts as the training window
grows by exactly one observation each week, for this fixed
`(order, seasonal_order)`. This is a cleaner, more specific understanding
than M1-011 alone provided, even though it rules out the specific fix
tested.

### Decision
**Reject warm-starting.** No accuracy benefit, and a real, substantial
performance cost. The `start_params`/`return_params`/`warm_start`
parameters are kept (harmless, off by default, and now-useful generic
infrastructure - e.g. a future ensemble/smoothing approach could still use
`return_params`), but `warm_start=False` remains the only path anyone
should use going forward. The underlying weekly-refit-instability question
remains open; untried candidates for future work include refitting on a
less frequent cadence (e.g. monthly, holding parameters fixed for
intermediate weeks) or smoothing/ensembling forecasts across the last few
weeks' independent refits, rather than trying to change what a single
week's optimizer converges to.

## Experiment ID: M1-014

### Date
2026-08-04

### Research Question
M1-013 ruled out "optimizer starting point" as the cause of weekly SARIMA
refit instability - the true MLE optimum itself appears to shift week to
week. Does refitting LESS often (e.g. every 4 weeks) - using
`SARIMAXResults.append(refit=False)` (a cheap Kalman-filter state update,
confirmed via the statsmodels docstring, NOT a re-optimization) to
incorporate new observations between refits without re-estimating
parameters - produce more stable, more fold-refit-like predictions than
refitting fresh every single week?

### Districts
`Kilinochchi`, `Colombo` (60-week cheap check, same districts/sample as
M1-013, before considering any full run).

### Stage 1 Model
New `rolling_one_step._low_freq_refit_step()`: cold `.fit()` every
`refit_interval_weeks` (4, i.e. monthly), `.append(new_obs, refit=False)`
in between, `.forecast(steps=1)` every week regardless of which path was
taken that week.

### Stage 2 Model
Not touched.

### Features Used
Unchanged.

### Metrics
Correlation and mean absolute difference between the low-frequency-refit
prediction and the fold-refit SARIMA prediction for the same
`(District, Year, Week)`, compared against the plain weekly cold-refit
baseline, on the same 60-week sample.

### Results
- **Per-fit cost**: substantially cheaper than weekly refitting, as
  expected (`Kilinochchi`: 60.1s total vs cold's 237.4s for the same 60
  weeks - roughly a 4x reduction, consistent with refitting only 1 week in
  4).
- **No meaningful stability improvement**: `Kilinochchi` correlation with
  fold-refit moved from -0.0518 (cold) to +0.0034 (low-freq) - both
  statistically indistinguishable from zero on this sample size, not a
  real improvement. `Colombo` correlation was effectively unchanged (0.1809
  cold vs 0.1833 low-freq); mean absolute difference from fold-refit was
  also essentially unchanged (61.25 vs 61.50).

### Residual Diagnostics
Not applicable.

### Interpretation
Refitting less often is cheaper (as expected - fewer full optimizations)
but does not address the underlying instability - `.append(refit=False)`
by construction keeps the SAME parameters from the last cold refit, so it
cannot correct for whatever caused that refit's own parameters to already
differ from the fold-refit vintage's. This is consistent with, and adds to,
M1-013's finding: the problem is in what a SINGLE fit's true optimum is for
a given window, not in how often a fit happens or how its optimizer is
seeded.

### Decision
**Reject** as a general stabilization strategy - no accuracy signal to
justify further testing at full scale, unlike M1-015 (tested in parallel,
see that entry). `_low_freq_refit_step()` is kept as working, reusable
infrastructure (a genuine refit-cadence control that some future use case
might still want for its cost savings alone) but not pursued further this
session.

## Experiment ID: M1-015

### Date
2026-08-04

### Research Question
Does averaging, in transformed space, this week's fresh SARIMA forecast for
week *t* with the forecasts that the last several weeks' OWN independently-
fitted SARIMA models make for the SAME target week *t* (each extended
forward via a cheap `.forecast(steps=h)` call, not refit) produce more
accurate rolling-mode forecasts than using a single week's fit alone? This
is a standard forecast-combination approach (averaging reduces variance
when individual forecasts have partly independent errors) - distinct from,
and not ruled out by, M1-013's rejected warm-start approach (which tried to
change what a single fit converges to, not combine several independent
fits).

### Districts
All 25 (after a cheap 6-district check - `Colombo`, `Gampaha`, `Kandy`,
`Ratnapura`, `Badulla` showed a consistent positive signal; `Kilinochchi`
was inconclusive due to near-zero counts - justified committing to a full
run).

### Stage 1 Model
New `rolling_one_step._vintage_ensemble_step()`: fresh weekly `.fit()`
(identical cost to the existing default path - no slowdown), but the
week's SARIMA prediction is the mean, in transformed (log1p or raw) space,
of this week's own 1-step forecast AND the last `ensemble_window - 1 = 3`
weeks' own independently-fitted models' forecasts extended forward to the
same target week. Leakage-safe by construction: every ingredient is a
forecast from a model fit using data strictly before the target week - nothing
uses information from week *t* or later.

### Stage 2 Model
Unchanged (frozen `xgboost_final_model.json`, as in the standard rolling
evaluator).

### Features Used
Unchanged.

### Metrics
Full 25-district rolling one-step DM test (`compute_dm_results_rolling`,
unchanged) and rolling sMAPE (`summarize_metrics`, `holdout_all` period),
compared against the M1-008 baseline (single-vintage weekly refit).
Computed via a new parallel driver, `scripts/
run_rolling_one_step_ensemble_parallel.py` (mirrors `run_rolling_one_step_
parallel.py`'s process-pool pattern; ~3,326s wall time for all 25
districts, comparable to the baseline run since per-district cost is
essentially unchanged).

### Results
- **Districts with Stage 2 helping (`mean_loss_diff > 0`): 10/25 (baseline)
  -> 24/25 (ensemble).** Only `Kilinochchi` (a known extreme-sparse,
  near-zero-count district) shows a negative `mean_loss_diff`, and it is
  tiny and not significant (`p=0.83`).
- **DM-significant districts: 2/25 (baseline) -> 3/25 (ensemble)**
  (`Batticaloa` `p=0.012`, `Galle` `p=0.017`, `Hambantota` `p=0.015`;
  `Colombo` at `p=0.052` narrowly misses conventional significance).
- **Rolling sMAPE improved for 22/25 districts**, several substantially:
  `Gampaha` 51.7% -> 43.9% (-15.1%), `Ratnapura` 53.5% -> 46.3% (-13.5%),
  `Badulla` 66.0% -> 58.2% (-11.8%), `Colombo` 37.9% -> 35.2% (-7.1%).
  Median rolling sMAPE across all 25 districts: 58.8% -> 56.8%. The only 3
  districts that got (marginally) worse - `Kilinochchi`, `Mannar`,
  `Mullaitivu` - are exactly the most extreme zero-inflated districts, all
  by <1 percentage point.
- Full tables: `outputs/metrics/module1/rolling_one_step_dm_test_ensemble.csv`,
  `outputs/metrics/module1/rolling_one_step_metrics_ensemble.csv`,
  `data/processed/module1/rolling_one_step_predictions_ensemble.csv`.

### Residual Diagnostics
Not applicable (point-forecast comparison only).

### Interpretation
This is the first genuine, broad-based accuracy improvement found across
the M1-007 through M1-015 investigation arc. The mechanism is exactly the
one classic forecast-combination theory predicts: each week's fresh MLE
fit has real, partly-independent noise (M1-011/M1-013's finding), and
averaging several such fits' opinions about the same target week
cancels out some of that noise without needing to fix what any single fit
converges to. Crucially, unlike M1-013's warm-start (which cost 5-10x more
per fit for no benefit), this costs essentially nothing extra - the
vintage models being averaged are fits the rolling evaluator was already
going to need for subsequent weeks; ensembling just reuses their cheap
`.forecast()` calls.

### Decision
**Accept.** This is a genuine, broad, low-cost improvement to rolling-mode
(deployment-faithful) forecast accuracy. Directly relevant to the actual
"predict next week using data up to now" production nowcast
(`forecast_future.run_nowcast()`, Decision 031): that pathway currently
uses a SINGLE SARIMA refit, so it does not yet benefit from this finding.
Recommended next step (not yet implemented this session, pending user
sign-off given the scope of touching the production nowcast path): extend
`run_nowcast()`/`forecast_future.py` to construct a small ensemble of
recent retrospective refits (e.g. fit on data through the last 4 available
weeks separately, each forecasting forward to the SAME target week) rather
than relying on a single fit, mirroring `_vintage_ensemble_step()`'s logic.

Unchanged.

### Features Used
Unchanged.

Absolute error vs. real outcomes, per (district, week), for single-fit vs.

  rules, not 3.)

Not applicable.

The bigger sample confirms the improvement is real but more modest (56.8%

### Research Question
A user reviewing the dashboard's 8-week case forecast noticed the numbers appeared to decline in
nearly every district and asked whether that reflects real epidemiological behavior. Investigate
directly: is the decline real signal, or an artifact of how `forecast_future.py`'s Stage 2 recursion

### Districts
All 25.

### Stage 1 Model
Not touched - used to isolate whether the decline originates here.

### Stage 2 Model
Not touched - used to isolate whether the decline originates here.

### Features Used
Not applicable - this is a diagnostic of the existing forecast output, not a new feature/model.

### Metrics
Decomposed `future_forecast.csv` into `sarima_prediction` (Stage 1) and `predicted_residual`

`feature_completeness_pct`, both already present in the output.

### Results
- **24 of 25 districts** show a lower `final_prediction` at horizon_step=8 than horizon_step=1;
  four (Kilinochchi, Mannar, Vavuniya, Mullaitivu) collapse to exactly 0 by step 8.
- **Stage 1 (SARIMA) stays essentially flat** across the full 8-step horizon - e.g. Colombo's

  The decline is not coming from Stage 1.
- **Stage 2's `predicted_residual` declines steadily**, and the mechanism is directly visible:
  `residual_lag_1_is_recursive` is `False` only at horizon_step=1 and `True` for every later step -

and more precise finding than the existing "error compounding" framing (`DASHBOARD_GUIDE.md`'s

worst effects (feature completeness floor rises from 47% to 64%; districts collapsing to exactly 0

textbook fix (separate direct per-horizon models instead of one model applied recursively) this
session - that is a real modeling change requiring its own walk-forward validation per horizon, not
something to bolt on without that validation this close to a defense. Flagged as a named, credible
follow-up rather than fixed or hidden.

## Experiment ID: M1-023

### Date
2026-08-11

### Research Question
M1-022/Decision 053 named the recursive multi-week forecast's downward bias but deliberately did
not fix it, flagging the textbook remedy (direct per-horizon models instead of one model applied
recursively) as a real but unvalidated candidate. Pilot the smallest piece of that: does a direct
h=2 Stage 2 model, walk-forward validated at genuinely dense (weekly, not just the 14 annual fold
boundaries) historical origins, actually beat the current recursive approach's h=2 output - and by
how much?

### Districts
All 25.

### Stage 1 Model
Unchanged SARIMA per-district configs (`sarima_selected_configs.csv`). New computation, not a new
model: for every historical origin week, one fit on data strictly before that origin, asked for
`n_periods=2` (cheap - `.forecast(steps=2)` from an already-converged fit, not a second
optimization) instead of the production pipeline's usual `n_periods=1`.

### Stage 2 Model
Three Stage 2 variants compared at every scored origin, all using the frozen production
`XGB_BASE_PARAMS` and the exact production fold-training recipe
(`train_and_predict_fold`/`train_and_predict_holdout`, unmodified):
1. **SARIMA-h2-alone** - no Stage 2 correction, the 2-step SARIMA forecast itself.
2. **Recursive-h2** - the CURRENT production model applied to the origin's real h1 feature row,
   then recursed one more step exactly as `forecast_future.py` already does (its own predicted h1
   output inserted as a synthetic case count, features rebuilt, model applied again).
3. **Direct-h2 (new)** - a SEPARATE model, walk-forward trained on an origin-anchored table where
   every feature is the origin week's own (real, never-recursive) value and only
   `sarima_prediction` is swapped for the genuine 2-step SARIMA forecast - never sees a synthetic
   lag value.

Two previously-undocumented leakage risks were found and avoided while designing the direct-h2
table (not obvious from the existing per-row feature table - see `direct_horizon_pilot.py`'s module
docstring for the full mechanism): `sarima_prediction` and the three climate-anomaly features are
both normally computed relative to a row's OWN week, which for a naively-reused feature row would
silently mean predicting further ahead than the features actually support.

### Features Used
Identical `FEATURE_COLUMNS` (21 features + District) for all three variants - the comparison
isolates the STRATEGY (recursive vs. direct), not the feature set.

### Validation Method
Reuses `compensation_model.py`'s exact 14 walk-forward folds (`compute_fold_boundaries`) and
holdout block, with fold assignment keyed on each row's ORIGIN week. MASE (m=52, scale from each
district's full case series), sMAPE, and Diebold-Mariano (squared loss) computed per district, on
`split ∈ {pre_holdout, holdout, all}`. 21,425 rolling-origin rows generated
(`scripts/run_direct_horizon_pilot_parallel.py`, ~48 min parallelized across districts); 19,375
scored (fold-trained, non-imputed).

### Results
- **Holdout (the confirmatory check, not the exploratory one) favors direct clearly**: median MASE
  - direct 0.391 vs. recursive 0.445 vs. SARIMA-alone 0.408; median sMAPE - direct 37.0% vs.
  recursive 40.6% vs. SARIMA-alone 39.2%. Direct beats recursive in **22/25 districts on MASE** and
  **23/25 on sMAPE**.
- **Pre-holdout (the larger, exploratory sample) is closer**: median MASE is essentially a tie
  (direct 0.479 vs. recursive 0.478), but direct still clearly beats recursive on median sMAPE
  (59.2% vs. 64.4%).
- **Diebold-Mariano**: only 2/25 districts reach `p<0.05` for direct vs. recursive (Badulla,
  Ratnapura) - both significant results favor direct (`mean_loss_diff > 0` in both, meaning direct
  has the lower loss); zero districts show recursive winning significantly. Most districts show no
  statistically significant difference either way at this sample size.
- **The four districts that collapsed to exactly 0 in the original 8-week recursive production
  forecast (Kilinochchi, Mannar, Vavuniya, Mullaitivu)**: direct beats recursive on holdout MASE and
  sMAPE for 3 of 4 (Mullaitivu, Vavuniya clearly; Kilinochchi on both metrics though SARIMA-alone
  beats both Stage-2 variants there, consistent with Kilinochchi's already-known M1-009/Decision
  034 regression under residual correction generally). Mannar is a genuine mixed/close case
  (recursive very slightly better on both metrics there).
- **Honest nuance, not hidden**: a raw (unweighted, cross-district-pooled) mean bias check shows
  direct's aggregate bias (+5.38) is actually LARGER than recursive's (+1.32) - but this pooled
  statistic is dominated by high-volume districts (Colombo, Gampaha) and is not scale-normalized,
  unlike MASE/sMAPE; it does not contradict the per-district-normalized result above, but is
  reported here rather than omitted.

### Interpretation
The holdout result - the stronger of the two checks by this project's own established convention -
supports the hypothesis directly: removing the recursive feedback loop (never letting Stage 2 feed
on its own prior prediction) measurably improves 2-week-ahead accuracy for most districts, and does
not make any district significantly worse. The pre-holdout near-tie on MASE (while still favoring
direct on sMAPE) suggests the improvement, while real, is not enormous at h=2 specifically - the
recursive bias only becomes severe at LATER horizons (h≥3, per M1-022's finding that the decline
compounds), so a bigger win is plausible, not yet confirmed, at h=3/h=4.

### Decision
**Accept the pilot's finding as genuine evidence that the direct strategy is worth pursuing
further; do NOT yet promote it to production.** This was scoped deliberately as a pilot (user
request, given the added complexity found while designing it) to answer "does this help" before

## Experiment ID: M1-024

### Date
2026-08-11

### Research Question
M1-023 piloted the direct strategy at h=2 only and found a holdout-confirmed improvement. Extend
the SAME pilot to h=3 and h=4 (generalizing `direct_horizon_pilot.py` to compute all three horizons
in one pass, chaining the recursive comparison through each step exactly as `forecast_future.py`
does). Working hypothesis going in (from M1-022's finding that the recursive bias compounds with
horizon): the direct model's advantage over the recursive approach should GROW, not shrink, at
h=3/h=4.

### Districts
All 25.

### Stage 1 Model
Unchanged. One SARIMA fit per historical origin, now asked for `n_periods=4` (was `n_periods=2` in
M1-023) - the dominant cost (the fit itself) is NOT repeated per horizon; only the cheap
`.forecast(steps=N)` extraction and the recursive feature-reconstruction/XGBoost-predict chain grow
with horizon.

### Stage 2 Model
Same three variants as M1-023 (SARIMA-alone, recursive, direct), now computed for h=2, 3, AND 4
together per origin, with the recursive comparison genuinely CHAINED (h3's synthetic history
includes h2's own recursive prediction, h4's includes h3's) - not three independent single-step
recursions. Same frozen production `XGB_BASE_PARAMS` and fold-training recipe as M1-023.

### Features Used
Identical `FEATURE_COLUMNS` for all three variants at all three horizons - origin-anchored base
feature row shared across horizons, only `sarima_prediction` differs per horizon (see
`direct_horizon_pilot.py`'s module docstring for the full mechanism, unchanged from M1-023).

### Validation Method
Same as M1-023 (14 walk-forward folds, holdout block, MASE/sMAPE/DM-test), run for h=2, 3, 4 in one
combined pass. 21,375 rolling-origin rows generated (~64 min parallelized across districts,
`scripts/run_direct_horizon_pilot_parallel.py`); 19,325 scored per horizon (fold-trained,
non-imputed). Found and fixed one bug while generalizing the eval script: the feature-column list
included `District` twice (once explicitly, once via `FEATURE_COLUMNS`, which already contains it),
silently producing a duplicate column that broke the fold-key `zip()` - caught immediately by a
crash, not a silent wrong result, when dry-testing against a small sample before the full run.

### Results
**The hypothesis was NOT confirmed - the direct model's advantage over recursive NARROWS with
horizon on holdout, rather than growing, though it never reverses in a way that reaches
significance:**

| Horizon | Direct beats recursive (MASE) | Direct beats recursive (sMAPE) | DM significant (25 districts) | Median holdout MASE (direct / recursive / SARIMA-alone) | Median holdout sMAPE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| h=2 | 22/25 | 22/25 | 2/25, both favor direct | 0.328 / 0.365 / 0.360 | 36.1% / 41.3% / 37.8% |
| h=3 | 22/25 | 23/25 | 1/25, favors direct | 0.408 / 0.409 / 0.403 | 39.8% / 44.6% / 42.9% |
| h=4 | 21/25 | 17/25 | 1/25, favors direct | 0.484 / 0.478 / 0.480 | 41.5% / 44.6% / 46.6% |

- District-level win rate stays a clear majority for direct at every horizon (never drops below
  17/25), but the sMAPE win rate specifically drops from 22-23/25 at h=2/h=3 to 17/25 at h=4.
- Median holdout MASE: direct's lead over recursive shrinks from +0.037 (h=2) to +0.001 (h=3) to
  **-0.006 (h=4, recursive marginally ahead)** - direct's MASE advantage effectively disappears by
  h=4, even though its sMAPE and district-win-rate advantage does not.
- **Zero districts, at any horizon, show recursive winning significantly against direct** - the DM
  test never flips in recursive's favor, even where the median metrics narrow to a near-tie.
- **A separate, equally real pattern**: ALL THREE approaches' absolute skill over naive SARIMA
  converges toward each other as horizon grows - e.g. SARIMA-alone's holdout MASE (0.360, 0.403,
  0.480) is close behind or ahead of BOTH Stage-2 variants by h=3/h=4, unlike at h=2 where direct
  clearly separates from SARIMA-alone (0.328 vs. 0.360). This suggests the honest limiting factor
  is not "recursive vs. direct" specifically, but that ANY Stage 2 correction - direct or recursive
  - has less to add over the statistical baseline as horizon grows, because the short-term signals
  (residual/case lags) that give Stage 2 its edge decay in relevance the further out the target is.
- Pre-holdout (the larger, exploratory sample) shows the same narrowing pattern, slightly earlier:
  direct leads on MASE at h=2 (0.479 vs. 0.486) but recursive is marginally ahead at h=3 (0.532 vs.
  0.552) and h=4 (0.597 vs. 0.609); direct still leads on sMAPE at h=2/h=3, and is close (recursive
  marginally ahead) at h=4.

### Interpretation
M1-022's finding that the recursive bias grows with horizon is still true on its own terms (verified
directly in the earlier decomposition of `future_forecast.csv`), but it does not translate into an
ever-widening DIRECT-vs-RECURSIVE gap the way the initial hypothesis assumed - because the
RECURSIVE model isn't the only thing losing ground at longer horizons; the DIRECT model's own edge
over plain SARIMA is *also* shrinking, for the more fundamental reason that less real, informative
signal survives that far from the forecast origin regardless of strategy. The honest, supportable
conclusion is narrower than "direct fixes the problem at every horizon": direct is a genuine,
holdout-confirmed improvement that is strongest and most reliable at h=2, remains a real (if
narrowing) improvement through h=3-4, and never loses to recursive in a way that reaches
significance at any horizon tested - but it does not fully close the gap between "how far can this
framework forecast with confidence" and "4 weeks," since even the best of the three approaches
degrades substantially by h=4 (median sMAPE 41-47% vs. ~22-38% at h=2, depending on approach).

### Decision
**Accept the pilot's finding; still do not promote to production.** The clearest, least-caveated
case for the direct strategy remains h=2 specifically (M1-023's original scope). h=3/h=4 show real
but weaker, more mixed support - genuinely useful evidence for the report (this is exactly the kind
of finding this project's own discipline calls for reporting honestly rather than only reporting the
horizon that looks best), but not grounds to claim the direct strategy is a broad fix for "the
multi-week forecast" as a whole. If this work continues toward production, h=2 alone is the
strongest, most defensible candidate; h=3/h=4 would need their own justification given how close the
three approaches become by that point.
