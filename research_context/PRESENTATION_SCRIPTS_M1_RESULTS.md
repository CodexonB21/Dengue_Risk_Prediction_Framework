## Slide — Results: Stage 1 vs Stage 1+2 (~30 sec)

On validation, residual compensation improved **MASE** — mean absolute scaled error — for **all twenty-five districts**. On holdout, the **median improvement** was about **forty-three percent** on validation and **thirty-two percent** on holdout. Median holdout MASE moved from about **0.62 to 0.37**. Examples on the slide — **Colombo, Gampaha, and Batticaloa** — show the same pattern: Stage 1+2 beats SARIMA alone.

## Slide — Results: Stage 1 vs Stage 1+2 Cont. · Figure 7.2 (~40 sec)

Figure 7.2 shows **Colombo and Gampaha** on the untouched holdout block. The **grey line** is actual weekly cases. The **dashed blue line** is Stage 1 SARIMA — it stays relatively flat and misses peaks. The **orange line** is Stage 1+2. It follows seasonal rises and falls much more closely. This is the visual proof that residual compensation recovers structured error the baseline leaves behind.

## Slide — Holdout MASE: All 25 Districts · Figure 7.3 (~35 sec)

Figure 7.3 summarises **all twenty-five districts**. Each grey point is Stage 1 MASE; each orange point is Stage 1+2. For most districts, the orange point shifts left — lower error. Improvement is visible in both high-burden and moderate-burden districts, confirming that residual compensation works **broadly across the country**, not only in Colombo and Gampaha.
