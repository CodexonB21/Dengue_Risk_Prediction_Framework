# Chapter 1 — Section 1.1 Introduction

## 1.1 Introduction

Mosquito-borne diseases, particularly dengue, remain a major public health and economic burden in tropical and subtropical regions. In Sri Lanka, dengue incidence exhibits complex seasonal and inter-annual patterns shaped by climate variability, urbanization, and human mobility, which can produce abrupt and difficult-to-anticipate outbreak peaks. Although vector-control programmes are in place, conventional disease management has often remained largely reactive: interventions intensify after case counts have already risen, rather than being guided by timely, district-specific risk intelligence. This delay is commonly linked to the limited availability of predictive decision-support tools that can anticipate risk at an operationally useful spatial and temporal resolution. As highlighted by Uduwanage et al. (2025), the complexity of dengue transmission requires advanced predictive approaches that go beyond simple historical averages if outbreaks are to be anticipated more effectively [1].

This research proposes a residual compensation modeling framework for dengue risk prediction that integrates three complementary analytical components: hybrid time-series case forecasting, hybrid outbreak risk classification, and hybrid spatial hotspot detection. The framework operates at the district-week level across Sri Lanka’s administrative districts and combines historical epidemiological incidence with meteorological covariates such as temperature, rainfall, and humidity. Rather than relying on a single baseline model, each module is designed so that systematic residual or calibration errors left by a baseline method can be learned and corrected using additional explanatory information. The integrated outputs are intended to support early-warning interpretation through a decision-support dashboard that visualizes predicted case burden, outbreak risk, and spatial concentration patterns, thereby helping public health planners move from reactive response toward more proactive risk-informed preparedness.
