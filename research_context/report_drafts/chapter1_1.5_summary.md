## 1.5 Summary

Chapter 1 established the need for a more proactive approach to dengue risk management in Sri Lanka. Although official surveillance already provides district-level weekly case data, existing practice remains largely reactive and often relies on fragmented predictive views that do not jointly support forecasting, outbreak-risk interpretation, and spatial prioritisation. The chapter identified a central methodological gap: baseline models frequently leave systematic residual or calibration errors, including errors associated with climate and contextual variation that are not adequately corrected in single-stage approaches.

The problem was framed around three linked limitations: incomplete multidimensional risk intelligence covering case magnitude, outbreak probability, and geographic concentration; insufficient residual compensation in baseline predictive methods; and weak translation of model outputs into usable early-warning decision support. In response, this research proposes a Residual Compensation Modeling Framework comprising three complementary modules: Hybrid Time-Series Case Forecasting, Hybrid Outbreak Risk Classification, and Hybrid Spatial Hotspot Detection. Each module follows a two-stage baseline-then-compensation design, although the meaning of compensation differs by task, ranging from residual case-count correction to probability calibration and spatial residual adjustment. The chapter therefore sets the foundation for the subsequent literature review and for the detailed presentation of the proposed framework, its design, implementation, and evaluation.
